# Writing style for the final report (Sudarshan's voice)

Taken from three of Sudarshan's published reviews (Gizmochina: OnePlus 13, OnePlus Nord 4, OnePlus Nord CE 4 Lite) and adapted to an academic report.
`scripts/check_report_style.py` checks every chapter against the "never" list below.

## Keep (from the samples)
- **Short, plain sentences.** Many paragraphs are one to three sentences. One idea per sentence. Everyday words over technical ones; when a technical term is needed, explain it once in plain words.
- **First person, from experience.** "I built", "I tested", "I expected", "I was surprised to find". Personal judgement is stated openly: "I believe", "Personally, I prefer", "in my view".
- **Simple connectors at the start of sentences:** However, Further, Also, Thus, Therefore, Additionally, Interestingly, Unsurprisingly, On top of this, As a result, Simply put, To be fair, Finally. Vary them; do not stack them.
- **Context first, then the details.** Open a chapter with the bigger picture (the Indian OTT market, the business problem), then narrow down.
- **Concrete numbers with units**, as in the spec-heavy reviews: "3.1 million ratings", "91 ms", "49.4 MiB". Numbers carry the argument, not adjectives.
- **Asides in brackets:** "(more on this in Chapter 6)", "(a simulation, not an A/B test)".
- **Numbered, descriptive headings** that say what the section is about: "3.2 Why I Did Not Split the Data at Random". An occasional question heading is fine: "Does Context Help?".
- **Honest verdicts that name the flaws.** Say plainly what did not work, and why ("The ranker is the bottleneck, not the database"). Misses are reported as misses.
- **A clear recommendation at the end of a discussion**, like a review verdict: who should use what, and when.

## Adapt for an academic report
- Past tense for what was done and found; present tense for what a table or figure shows.
- APA 7th: in-text (Author, Year) or Author (Year); a reference list at the end. Every source is a real, well-known publication or official documentation, flagged for Sudarshan to verify.
- Tables and figures are numbered by chapter (Table 3.1, Figure 5.2) and referred to in the text.
- Slang from the samples ("Anyhoo", "cheaped out") is left out; the tone stays friendly but formal enough for an MBA panel.
- American spelling, as in the samples (color, analyze, optimize).

## Never
- **No em dashes or en dashes** as punctuation. Use a comma, a full stop, brackets or a colon. Ranges are written with "to": "0.0229 to 0.0261".
- **No stock AI phrasing.** Banned words and phrases: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, robust, leverage, seamless(ly), crucial, pivotal, vital, paramount, comprehensive, nuanced,
  intricate, underscore(s), showcase(s), navigate, elevate, unlock, harness, foster, testament, holistic, game-changer, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, transformative,
  meticulous(ly), embark, journey, ever-evolving, "in today's", "it is worth noting", "it's important to note", notably, furthermore, "in conclusion", "in summary",
  "not only ... but also" (at most once in the whole report), myriad, plethora, "stands as", "serves as a", boasts, "let's", "dive", "deep dive", "key takeaway",
  "valuable insights", vibrant, empower, streamline, synergy, paradigm, noteworthy, commendable, remarkable, ultimately, "plays a role", "a wide range of", "in the world of".
- No rule-of-three adjective lists ("fast, reliable and scalable") as decoration.
- No opening summaries like "This chapter will explore..."; just start.
- No closing lines that restate the paragraph.
- "Significant" only in the statistical sense, and then say what the interval is.
