WHY NOT REPLAY: the replay estimator of Li et al. (2010) needs logged actions chosen at random (or with known probabilities) and keeps only events where the logged action equals the policy's pick. MovieLens ratings are users' own choices steered by the site, and with ~3341 arms nearly every logged event would be thrown away. So: a semi-synthetic simulation.
arms: 3341 new releases (TMDB release since cutoff-6mo, no ratings before 2015-02-03); hidden like-rate of an arm = a draw from Beta(rate*5 + likes, (1-rate)*5 + n - likes) built from its real ratings in the validation window (global rate 0.457); a fresh draw per run and seed.
requests: 31909 real session starts (30-min rule) from 3308 users between 2015-02-03 and 2017-03-28; a request shows k lane slots; an arm is on the shelf from its TMDB release date for 365 days (180 as sensitivity).
content prior (centered SBERT -> like-rate, ridge, fitted on 6967 PRE-cutoff movies with >= 20 ratings): cross-validated correlation 0.520; empirical-Bayes weight 8.0 pseudo-ratings. Prior-only = always show the arms with the best prior.
30 seeds per setting; per seed every strategy sees the SAME hidden like-rates and reward coins (paired). Intervals are 95% over seeds.

== WHAT COULD MAKE THIS SIMULATION MISLEADING ==
1. Like-rates come from people who chose to rate the film, so they are probably higher than for a viewer shown the film by a lane; the reward ignores click probability.
2. One hidden rate per arm for everybody: real taste varies by user, so a non-contextual bandit undervalues niche films (LinUCB-style methods are the next step).
3. The main arm set needs >= 5 ratings, i.e. films that already attracted viewers; real duds are missing, which makes the problem easier. The 2,031-arm set is heavily shrunk toward the global rate.
4. No position bias, fatigue, repeat exposure or real viewing behaviour; every impression is instantly judged.
5. Rates are stationary within the window; real interest decays after release.
6. The content prior was learned on older films and may transfer imperfectly to new ones.
7. Intervals reflect seeds (truth draws and coin flips), not modelling choices; hidden rates are themselves estimated from thin data.
8. The slot-cost comparison mixes real held-out likes in one evaluation with simulated likes in another.
